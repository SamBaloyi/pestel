Q1 — Business Cycles & Recession23 marks

4 sub-parts: recession effects (8), macro-indicator matching (5), True/False on a cycle diagram (5), yield curve (5).

Two labels for the same cycle — know both

Your Chapter 5 slides use Recovery → Boom → Slump → Recession. This revision guide (closer to the actual paper) uses Expansion → Peak → Contraction → Trough → Recovery. They describe the same fluctuation — go with the guide's wording for definitions and diagram labelling since it's built from the paper, but recognise either if it shows up.

The five phases (paper's terminology)

Expansion

Economic activity increasing across the board.

Peak

High point before turning downward. Capacity pressure & inflation can be very high here.

Contraction

Overall economic activity visibly decreasing.

Trough

Absolute low point before activity begins to recover.

Recovery

Economic activity rises again after the trough.

Reading a labelled cycle diagram

The paper's own example: points B and F are peaks; D and H are troughs. A rising section = expansion/recovery; a falling section = contraction.
📌 True/False technique

Before answering, check the exact location of the named point — don't confuse a trough with a point partway up the following upswing. Statements will test whether you can tell a labelled point/section apart from a similar-looking neighbour.

Business-cycle indicators

TypeTimingExample (from the guide)
LeadingChanges before overall activity — predicts the futureJSE All Share Index

Imports move closely with domestic activity; vehicle & retail sales directly reflect consumer spending. The guide's own rule: don't classify an indicator by whether it rises or falls — classify it by WHEN it changes relative to activity.

Exam framing

Questions ask for the "likely effect" — answer strictly in the context of a significant slowdown, and always give the direction and a short accurate reason. A direction alone won't get full marks.

Yield curve (5 marks)

Positively sloped: yield rises as maturity increases. Longer maturities command higher yields.

A yield curve plots bond yields against maturity. To compare borrowing costs: a lower yield = a lower stated cost of borrowing at that maturity. On a positively sloped curve, short-term borrowing is cheaper than long-term borrowing.

Q2 — Money, Credit & Monetary Policy19 marks

3 sub-parts: matching concepts to descriptions (7), a 75bps repo-hike scenario + credit-market graph (9), naming 3 functions of money (3).

Money multiplier

Formula

Maximum multiplier = 1 ÷ Reserve Ratio (as a decimal)

📌 Worked example (guide's own numbers)

Reserve ratio = 5% → decimal = 0.05 → multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20. Key assumption: banks hold no excess reserves (the maximum-potential case).

Your Chapter 6 slides use a different example (20% reserve ratio → multiplier of 5, R100 → R500 total money creation). Same formula, different numbers — practice plugging in whatever ratio the paper gives you rather than memorising one answer.

Contractionary vs expansionary policy

Contractionary (reduces liquidity)Expansionary (increases liquidity)
Selling government securitiesBuying government securities
Raising the repo/policy rateLowering the repo/policy rate or reserve requirement
MCQ strategy

For any policy-action MCQ: ask "does this increase or decrease liquidity and borrowing costs?" That single question resolves almost every option.

Money demand & the equilibrium interest rate

Equilibrium is where money demanded = money supplied (vertical axis: interest rate; horizontal axis: quantity of money). A shift in money demand is different from a movement along the curve — know the distinction.

Change (money supply unchanged)Effect on equilibrium interest rate
Money demand increasesRises
Money demand decreasesFalls

75-basis-point repo hike scenario

Scenario: inflationary pressure rises sharply, SARB raises the repo rate 75bps. This is restrictive/contractionary policy aimed at reducing excessive demand and anchoring inflation expectations.

  1. Higher repo rate — immediately raises the cost of short-term funds
  2. Increased market rates — banks pass this through to lending rates for consumers & businesses
  3. Economic contraction — higher borrowing costs reduce credit demand, consumption, and investment
⚠️ Draw the SHIFT correctly — this is a supply-side model

In this guide's version of the credit-market graph, a tighter monetary stance shifts the supply curve (S₁ → S₂, leftward/up) — not demand. Result: equilibrium moves from E₁ (r₁, q₁) to E₂ (r₂, q₂), where the interest rate rises (r₂ > r₁) and quantity of credit falls (q₂ < q₁).

Supply shifts left (S₁→S₂) under restrictive policy: equilibrium moves up and left — rate up, quantity down.

Three functions of money (3 marks)

  • Medium of exchange — accepted in payment for goods and services
  • Unit of account — a common measure for expressing prices and values
  • Store of value — allows purchasing power to be carried into the future
Don't lose easy marks here

Name three distinct functions clearly — don't accidentally give three examples of the same function.

International TradeSection A only

Not covered in your Chapter 5/6/8 slides at all — this is new content from the revision guide. MCQ-only, but don't skip it: it's worth marks in Section A.

Comparative advantage

  • Based fundamentally on the lowest opportunity cost — not on who is more productive overall
  • Opportunity cost = the quantity of one product you must give up to produce more of another
  • A country can hold a comparative advantage even if another country is more productive in absolute terms
  • Trade lets countries specialise in their advantage and exchange efficiently
📌 MCQ clue

If a question says a country "gives up fewer units of another product" to produce something — that's pointing you to comparative advantage.

Trade protection

Distinguish protection (quotas/tariffs) from export restrictions and subsidies — the guide flags this as a common confusion point.

SACU & regional trade

SACU membership — memorise exactly

Botswana, Eswatini, Lesotho, Namibia, South Africa (the "BLNS" countries + South Africa).

Customs union = common external trade arrangements established among member nations. The paper's focus is primarily on accurately recognising SACU membership and distinguishing SACU from other regional groupings.

Public good — traditionally has two properties: non-rivalry and non-excludability. Non-exclusion means it's difficult/impossible to stop people benefiting once it's provided.

📌 Exact matching clue

The phrase "good if provided to one is provided to all" points straight to the exclusion principle.

Developmental state — an active government role specifically guiding, supporting, and driving economic development through broad development planning. Clue phrase: "development planning is essential for government."
