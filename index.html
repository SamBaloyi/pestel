Chapter 4
External Environmental Analysis · pp. 107–140
4.1 The 3 Layers of the External Environment ▼
Key purpose: Identify opportunities (can exploit) and threats (must avoid or reduce). Includes 4 interrelated activities: scanning, monitoring, forecasting, and assessing.
1. Global Environment
Broad international forces — globalisation, trade agreements (AGOA/TIFA), international competition, speed, innovation, knowledge management
2. Macro Environment
PESTLE forces — Political, Economic, Sociocultural, Technological, Ecological. Organisations CANNOT directly control these.
3. Industry/Market Environment
Porter's Five Forces — the competitive structure of the specific industry the org operates in
4.2 PESTLE — The Macro Environment (Visual) ▼
🏛️
Political
Taxes, labour laws, regulations, lockdowns, AGOA, trade agreements, government stability
💰
Economic
Interest rates, inflation, GDP, unemployment, exchange rates, VAT (15%), recession, consumer expenditure
👥
Sociocultural
Cultural values, demographics, consumer trends (e.g. healthy food), social media influence, SA subcultures
💻
Technological
New products, processes, digital disruption, 4IR, innovation — no company is insulated from tech change
🌿
Ecological
Human-environment relationship, limited natural resources, sustainability obligations, ESG considerations
⚖️
Legal
Legislation, compliance, regulatory risks, policy shifts — Labour Relations Act, Consumer Protection Act, etc.
In the slides this is called PESTE (no separate L) but your textbook uses PESTEL/PESTLE — know both names refer to the same framework.
4.3 Porter's Five Forces Model (Visual) ▼
Used to analyse the industry/competitive environment. The stronger the forces = the more intense competition = lower profitability for firms in the industry.
🆕
Threat of New Entrants
Economies of scale · Brand loyalty · Capital req · Access to distribution · Government regulations
🏭
Bargaining Power of Suppliers
No. of suppliers · Uniqueness · Switching costs · Supplier concentration · Forward integration threat
⚔️
Competitive Rivalry
No. of competitors · Market growth · Differentiation · Switching costs · Exit barriers
🛒
Bargaining Power of Buyers
Buyer concentration · Price sensitivity · Standardisation · Availability of substitutes · Switching costs
🔄
Threat of Substitutes
Availability · Relative price-performance · Switching costs · Buyer willingness · Innovation enabling substitutes

Common Mistakes When Identifying Competitors

  • 1Over-emphasising current known competitors, ignoring potential new entrants or international competitors
  • 2Focusing only on large competitors, overlooking threat of small competitors
  • 3Assuming competitors will continue to behave the same way as in the past
4.4 Key Success Factors (KSFs) for an Industry ▼
KSFs = the strategy elements, product attributes, competitive capabilities, or intangible assets with the greatest impact on future success in a specific marketplace.
  • 1Strong customer relationships
  • 2Efficient cost management
  • 3Product/service quality and innovation
  • 4Skilled and motivated workforce
  • 5Effective distribution and supply chain
  • 6Brand reputation and market position
Case study: Spur Restaurant KSFs (see textbook pp. 138–140).
4.5 EFE Matrix — External Factor Evaluation ▼
The EFE Matrix is a tool to summarise and evaluate how well an organisation is responding to opportunities and threats in the external environment.

How to Build an EFE Matrix

  • 1List key Opportunities (positive external factors)
  • 2List key Threats (negative external factors)
  • 3Assign a Weight (0.0–1.0) based on importance — all weights must total 1.0
  • 4Assign a Rating (1–4) for how well the org responds: 4=superior, 3=above avg, 2=average, 1=poor
  • 5Calculate Weighted Score = Weight × Rating for each factor
  • 6Sum all weighted scores = Total Weighted Score
FactorWeightRatingScore
More kids in town (Opportunity)0.341.2
New competitor opening (Threat)0.220.4
School banning sugar (Threat)0.310.3
Holiday season (Opportunity)0.230.6
Total1.02.5/4
Score of 2.5/4 = moderately good external response. Above 2.5 = strong response; below 2.5 = weak response to external factors.
Chapter 5
Internal Environmental Analysis · pp. 141–161
5.1 Why Internal Analysis? Internal vs External ▼
Internal Analysis tells you:
What the organisation CAN do — identifies strengths and weaknesses in resources, capabilities, processes and culture
External Analysis tells you:
What the organisation MAY CHOOSE to do — identifies opportunities and threats in the environment
An organisation is a bundle of resources, capabilities and core competencies that must be used to create an exclusive competitive position in the market.

Areas Examined in Internal Analysis

  • 1Resource Analysis — financial, physical, human, technological resources
  • 2Capability Assessment — skills, processes, competencies that enable resource use
  • 3Value Chain Analysis — primary and support activities that add value
  • 4Core Competency Identification — unique strengths that create competitive advantage
  • 5Org Culture & Structure — how leadership style & design affect performance & strategy execution
  • 6Financial Performance Review — profitability, liquidity, efficiency ratios
5.2 SWOT Analysis (Visual) ▼
💪 Strengths (Internal +)
Resources or capabilities that give a relative advantage over competitors. What the org does well.
⚠️ Weaknesses (Internal -)
Lack of or deficiency in a resource — creates a relative disadvantage vs competitors.
🌟 Opportunities (External +)
Favourable situations in the organisation's external (macro + market) environment to exploit.
⚡ Threats (External -)
Unfavourable situations in the organisation's external environment that must be managed.
⚠️ Limitations of SWOT: Subjectivity, oversimplification, static snapshot — does not prioritise factors or suggest strategies automatically.
5.3 Resource-Based View (RBV) — Resources, Capabilities & VRIN ▼
The RBV holds that an organisation's internal resources are more important than industry structure in gaining competitive advantage. Resources determine efficiency and effectiveness.

3 Categories of Resources

1. Tangible Assets
Easiest to identify — visible: location, buildings, equipment, financial assets, physical resources
2. Intangible Assets
Cannot touch — most powerful competitive advantage: reputation, brand name (Coca-Cola), patents, knowledge, culture
3. Organisational Capabilities
Complex network of processes & skills determining how inputs are transformed into outputs — often lies in employees' knowledge & functional expertise

VRIN Test — What Makes a Resource Valuable?

V
Valuable
Does it enable the org to exploit opportunities or neutralise threats? Must create real value for customers.
R
Rare
Is it unique or scarce? If all competitors have it, it cannot provide competitive advantage.
I
Inimitable
Is it difficult to copy or imitate? Must be hard to replicate through history, culture, social complexity.
N
Non-substitutable
Is there no strategic equivalent? Competitors must not be able to achieve similar outcomes another way.
Resources and capabilities must be unique and capable of leading to a sustainable competitive advantage to pass the VRIN test.
5.4 Value Chain Analysis (VCA) — Visual ▼
The value chain looks at all activities of an org to determine where value is actually added. Value = the amount customers are willing to pay for what the org provides. Activities are grouped into Primary and Support.

Primary Activities (direct value creation → the product/service)

📥
Input Logistics
Receiving, storing, distributing inputs
⚙️
Operations
Transforming inputs into final product
📤
Output Logistics
Distribution of product/service to customers
📣
Marketing
Inducements to get customers to buy
🛎️
Customer Service
Maintain value of product after sale

Support Activities (enable primary activities)

🛒
Procurement
Quality & speed of inputs
💡
Tech Development
Processes & equipment across chain
👥
HR Management
Recruit, select, train, compensate — affects ALL levels
🏢
Administration
Infrastructure for overall org goals
💵
Financial Mgmt
Effective financial recording & control
3 ways value is delivered to customer: (1) Product is unique/different, (2) Product is cheaper than competitors, (3) Org can respond quickly to customer needs.
5.5 IFE Matrix — Internal Factor Evaluation ▼
The IFE Matrix summarises and evaluates an org's internal strengths and weaknesses. Mirror image of the EFE Matrix but focuses internally.

How to Build an IFE Matrix (same structure as EFE)

  • 1List key Strengths and Weaknesses
  • 2Assign a Weight (0.0–1.0) — total must = 1.0
  • 3Assign a Rating: Strengths = 4 (major) or 3 (minor); Weaknesses = 2 (minor) or 1 (major)
  • 4Calculate Weighted Score = Weight × Rating
  • 5Total above 2.5 = internally strong; below 2.5 = internally weak
Case studies: Cashbuild (internal analysis) & Shoprite Checkers cohesion case study.
Chapter 6
Strategy Formulation: Long-term Goals & Generic Strategies · pp. 166–184
6.1 Long-term Goals — SMART Requirements ▼
Long-term goals are determined in line with the org's vision. They direct the strategy and must position the org differently from competitors.
S — Specific
Clearly understood, decisive and attainable overall objectives
M — Measurable
Must indicate a time frame and be quantifiable
A — Attainable
Ensures continued viability — must be realistic
R — Relevant
Consistent and congruent across all organisational units
T — Time-bound
Must specify a time frame within which they are to be achieved

To position itself differently from competitors, an org must decide whether to perform activities differently OR perform different activities.

6.2 Competitive Advantage ▼
Competitive advantage = what distinguishes an org from competitors. Achieved through distinctive competencies (special capabilities, technologies or resources) that competitors cannot readily copy.

For a competitive advantage to be valid, it must:

  • 1Relate to an attribute that has value and relevance for customers
  • 2Be perceived by the customer as a competitive advantage
  • 3Be sustainable — competitors cannot easily imitate it

6.3 Cost Leadership Strategy — Full Detail ▼
Sell to a broad market at the lowest possible cost. Products are standardised, not customised. Goal: out-manage rivals in efficiency.

How to achieve cost advantage:

  • →Out-manage rivals with efficient value chain activities
  • →Revamp value chain to eliminate/bypass cost-producing activities

Cost drivers to manage:

Economies of scale Learning curve effects Capacity utilisation Technological advances Supply chain management

Best when:

  • ✓Price competition is vigorous among rivals
  • ✓Target customer is price sensitive
  • ✓Products are similar (standardised) with little differentiation
  • ✓Market is large enough to provide economies-of-scale
  • ✓Buyers have high bargaining power

Advantages:

Increase market shareCustomer loyaltyKeeps new entrants out

Pitfalls:

Overly aggressive price-cutting → low profitEasy to imitateStill needs some differentiation
6.4 Differentiation Strategy — Full Detail ▼
Create something perceived as unique and valued by a broad market. Charge a premium price. Compete on uniqueness, not cost.

Forms of differentiation:

Prestige/Brand (BMW) Technology (LG) Innovation (Apple) Features (Microsoft) Customer service (Investec) Reliability (J&J) Unique taste (Nando's) Speed (Outsurance)

Best when:

  • ✓Buyers' preferences are diverse and varied
  • ✓Fewer competitors follow the same differentiation approach
  • ✓Many ways to differentiate and buyers value those differences
  • ✓Technology changes frequently, competition centres on product features
  • ✓Brand loyalty exists and durable uniqueness cannot be quickly imitated

Key to sustaining:

Constant innovation to stay ahead of imitative competitors.

Pitfalls:

Uniqueness not valuable Too much differentiation Premium too high Easily imitated Brand dilution Innovation costs outweigh price premium
6.5 Focus Strategy — Full Detail ▼
Concentrate on a narrow market niche. Within that niche, compete either on cost (cost focus) or on differentiation (differentiation focus).
Cost Focus
Serve a niche at lower cost than rivals serve it. Example: budget travel insurance for backpackers only.
Differentiation Focus
Serve a niche with something unique that broad competitors cannot match. Example: Porsche (sports cars only), eBay (online auctions), Treehouse.

Best when:

  • ✓Target niche is large enough to be profitable and offers good growth
  • ✓Allows smaller org to avoid direct competition with large players
  • ✓Customers willing to pay high premium for specialised product/service
  • ✓Customers are brand loyal and unlikely to switch regardless of price

Pitfalls:

Niche preferences shift to mainstream Competitors redefine the niche with new tech Niche becomes flooded with rivals
6.6 Best-Cost Strategy (Hybrid) ▼
Best-Cost = simultaneous cost leadership + differentiation. Target: value-conscious buyers who want quality features at a reasonable price. Competitive advantage is most difficult for competitors to imitate.
Strategic target
Value-conscious buyers
Basis of advantage
Ability to give customers MORE VALUE FOR MONEY
Product line
Appealing attributes + upscale features
Key to sustaining
Unique expertise managing costs DOWN while incorporating upscale features simultaneously

Best when:

  • ✓Customers are simultaneously price AND quality sensitive
  • ✓Economies of scale and learning exist in the market
  • ✓Competition is fierce and barriers to entry low
  • ✓Mass customisation becomes possible through advanced tech

Pitfalls:

Fail both cost AND quality → "stuck in middle" Underestimate combined challenge Miscalculate revenue sources
